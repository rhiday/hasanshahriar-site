## Where your niche actually fits (the whitespace)

Picture two axes:

- **Horizontal:** *tactical/operator* ←→ *personal/identity*
- **Vertical:** *insider/credentialed* ←→ *outsider/self-made*

Most of who you read clusters in two corners. The AI-and-product crowd (Lenny, a16z, Pragmatic Engineer) sits **tactical + insider**. The honest essayists (Ask Polly, The Workaround, Millerd) sit **personal + outsider** but they're *not builders* — they left the building game or were never in it.

**Your corner — personal + outsider AND still actively building — is nearly empty.** You're a CTO shipping real product (Zipli, in pilots with the City of Helsinki), running an agency, *and* writing with an essayist's honesty about the freeze and the immigrant double-vision. Millerd writes about leaving the path; you write from inside the arena, far from the center, still swinging. Visakan is the closest neighbor, and he's a different culture and lane.

**That's the position:** *the founder still in the ring who tells the truth about it — from the edge, not the center.* Tactical people can't go there (too exposed); pure essayists can't go there (no receipts). You can.

## What's realistically reachable

Be honest with yourself here, because the freeze loves a grandiose target it can never be judged against (you wrote that essay).

- **Don't aim at Lenny/Tim Urban scale.** That's the lottery-ticket trap.
- **Aim at the Millerd/Visakan tier:** a few thousand of the *right* readers — outsider builders, immigrant founders, talented people who freeze — who feel *seen* and tell one friend. That's a reachable 12–18 month target and it's the tier that actually converts into the reputation payoff (warm intros, partners, Makeshift trust).
- **First 1,000 true readers > 50,000 passersby.** One *Sent, Not Saved* shared peer-to-peer beats a viral hit that bounces.

## Steal these — concrete formats for your next essays

1. **One napkin sketch per essay (the Tim Urban move).** Ugly on purpose. A hand-drawn diagram of "the freeze at 95%," or "the two voices" (the uncle vs. *lokey ki bolbe*). This becomes your visual signature and it costs you a pen, not design chops. Highest-leverage single change.
2. **Morgan Housel discipline: one idea, one story, plain words.** Audit each draft — if it carries two theses, split it into two essays. Feeds your cadence *and* cures the polish-spiral.
3. **Concrete-or-cut (Harry Dry).** Every abstract claim gets a specific moment or gets deleted. You already do this at your best ("every email that goes out and doesn't end my life is one more data point").
4. **Own the phrase (Millerd).** Pick the one line that is unmistakably yours and repeat it across site, Substack, bio. *Sent, not saved* is the frontrunner — it's a verb, a value, and a cure in three words.
5. **Name the unspoken thing (Ask Polly).** Open or close each essay by naming a feeling the reader hasn't admitted. That's the line people screenshot and share.
6. **The outsider lens as a recurring device.** The double-vision (Bangladesh ↔ Helsinki, builder ↔ writer) is your most-owned territory. Use it as a *lens on building*, not just memoir — that's what keeps Tier-C operators reading too.
