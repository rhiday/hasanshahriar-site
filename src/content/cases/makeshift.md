Makeshift began less like a company and more like a habit: someone had a problem, I knew how to move it forward, and we started.

Over time that habit became a studio. The work crossed Webflow builds, migrations, technical SEO, ecommerce, content systems, CRM integration and custom software. The client history now contains **16 evidenced relationships**: direct clients alongside partner work through agencies and consultancies.

The range is useful. The pattern underneath it is more useful.

## Two kinds of trust

Direct clients such as Arkken and KONE produced depth. The work expanded over time, crossed disciplines and became part of a larger operating system.

Partner channels produced repetition. Agencies such as Yaser.uk and Netguru needed someone who could enter an existing client relationship, understand the constraints quickly and deliver without making the partnership harder to manage.

Those are different commercial models, but they depend on the same thing: being safe to hand an unclear problem to.

## What I learned by doing everything

Running a small studio removes the comforting borders between roles.

You scope, sell, design, build, explain, invoice, repair and sometimes admit that the original plan was wrong. You learn which details clients care about, which details users notice and which details only builders congratulate each other for.

That experience is why I work comfortably across product, engineering and go-to-market today. The range did not come from collecting skills. It came from being responsible for the whole outcome.

## The operating lesson

The best version of Makeshift was never “an agency that can do many things.”

It was a compact team around a problem: enough product judgement to find the real issue, enough technical range to build the answer, and enough ownership to remain after the clean part of the work was over.
