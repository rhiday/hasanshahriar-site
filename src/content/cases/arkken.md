The first Arkken project was a website job. The later work was about what happened after someone arrived.

Arkken sells high-consideration kitchens and storage furniture made in Finland. The buying journey is visual and emotional, but the sale itself is consultative. A visitor does not simply add a kitchen to a cart. They explore, configure, ask, wait and eventually speak to a person.

That makes the space between the website and the sales team unusually important.

## From page delivery to product flow

The relationship developed across several phases: landing and product pages, CMS structures, a product configurator, and a request-a-quote flow connected to HubSpot.

The interesting part was not putting a form on a page. It was preserving enough context for the sales conversation to begin properly.

A product choice, configuration and customer request needed to travel together into the CRM. When the available Zapier configuration reached its limits, I wrote the custom automation needed to complete the route.

## The real design problem

For a considered purchase, “conversion” is not a bright button. It is continuity.

The customer should not have to repeat the journey they just completed on the site. The salesperson should not receive a context-free notification. The business should be able to see what entered the system and what happened next.

That reframed the work from page production into a small revenue system:

1. Help someone understand the product.
2. Capture the choices that matter.
3. Route those choices into the sales workflow.
4. Give the team a useful place to continue.

## The operating lesson

The strongest client relationships expand sideways.

You start by doing the thing you were asked to do. If you stay close enough, you eventually see the adjacent problem that nobody put in the brief. In this case, the website was not separate from sales. It was the first room of the sales process.

That shift—from building pages to understanding the system around them—is the work I want more of.
