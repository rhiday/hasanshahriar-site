My uncle never wrote a business plan. My father didn't keep a laundry list. They saw a thing, they wanted it, they pulled in the people they liked, and they moved. That was the whole method. As a kid in the gramer bari I watched it work, and I copied it without knowing I was copying anything — I built things because building felt like the natural thing a person does with a day.

Then I became an immigrant and picked up a second voice. The one that never sleeps. The one that asks, before I do anything, *lokey ki bolbe* — what will people say. In Finland I was sure the real entrepreneurs were elsewhere, doing it the proper Western way, and that I was an outsider with my nose against the glass. So I stopped trusting the gut that had already built fifty things, and I started making lists instead. Invoices. Checklists. Job applications I found boring. I told myself this was being serious. It was being afraid.

Here's the thing I saw this weekend that I keep wanting to say plainly: the list was never the work. When I actually built companies, I didn't plan them into existence. I followed the gut, took the action, worked with people I liked. That last part — working with people I like — is the most Bangladeshi instinct I have, and it's also the most underrated strategy I own. It isn't soft. It's the engine. Every venture of mine that moved, moved because there were people in it I'd choose to sit with anyway.

The trap was thinking the second voice was smarter than the first. It isn't. It's just louder, and newer, and it speaks the limited language of *what will they think*. The gut speaks an older language — the one the animal speaks, the one with no opinion about whether you're allowed. The skill isn't silencing the second voice. It's to stop letting it sign the cheques.

So entrepreneurship was never the costume I was failing to wear. It's the sport. The one game I've been playing my whole life without scoring it. Keep the gut driving, keep the list in the glovebox where it belongs, pick the people first.

I'm not 40, waiting to begin. I started when I was a kid. I just forgot I was already on the field.
